01 / Overview

Cricket For All (CFA) is a cricket hub in Adelaide's Northern Suburbs offering coaching, lane hire, tours, academies, retail, and bat repairs. Its indoor facility operates year-round with heated and air-conditioned nets, making it the primary winter training option for club and rep cricketers in the region.

I interned with CFA through Adlab, a marketing agency that manages CFA's marketing. My title was Marketing & Content Creation Intern; my actual role was running the content operation. For five weeks I was the only person producing social media content for the account.

The brief was broad but the problem was specific: CFA's Instagram was inconsistent, and TikTok was roughly 90% retail content. Strong posts existed but sat alongside too many static tiles and pure sales posts that earned no reach beyond existing followers. The task was to audit what was working, write a strategy that fixed the imbalance, and produce enough content to prove the strategy in practice.

The Audit

Before writing any strategy I audited everything CFA had posted across Instagram and TikTok. The findings split cleanly into what was working and what wasn't.

What Worked

FormatWhy it performed
Educational content with pricesPeople want specific dollar figures. Named price points drove views and positioned CFA as an expert.
High-quality player footage with contextNet footage overlaid with "100kph" got significantly more traction than the same footage without numbers. Numbers turn footage into a story.
Trending formats, timed correctlyThe Valentine's Day "Wear your best" post worked because it was timely. Travis Head bat edit worked because it leveraged a news moment.

The "Never Heard of CFA" Test

"What is in this for someone who has never heard of CFA?"

Every post on the feed is also being seen by strangers who don't follow the account yet. If a stranger has no reason to engage with a post, the post is leaking reach. This test sits at the front of the pre-post checklist and is the single most important filter in the framework.

Feed / Story / Highlights Framework

The most consistently broken rule in CFA's existing behaviour was using these three surfaces interchangeably. Each has a distinct job.

Feed
Inform and attract

Longer, informative, detail-rich captions. Strong hooks. Must pass the "never heard of CFA" test. The primary surface for reaching new audiences. Target: 4 to 6 posts per week.

Audience temperature: cold and warm.

Story
Remind and convert

Short, punchy, urgency-focused. Share the feed post to story, never reformat the same idea twice. The production-time saver is the rule: post to feed first, then share that post to story.

Audience temperature: warm only.

Highlights
Make the permanent findable

Facilities, staff, services, FAQs. Eight Highlights planned: Tournaments, Academies, 1-on-1 Coaching, Tours, Retail, Bat Repairs, Meet the Team, Facility. The biggest underused asset on the account.

Audience temperature: varies. Often first touchpoint.

03 / Copywriting

Copywriting

Sole work. Over 15 Instagram captions across all five content pillars, 2 full video scripts for coaching staff to deliver to camera, and 5 hook variations written per coaching video for Lewis and Ashley to select from.

Sole work, April – May 2026

Voice and Tone Framework

CFA's voice is friendly, conversational, and inclusive. The brand's name says it plainly: Cricket for All means exactly that. The voice has to work for a U8 player's parent in the morning and a state-pathway rep cricketer in the afternoon. Approachable enough for a complete beginner picking up a bat for the first time, credible enough for an elite junior who trains daily and follows the game seriously. Both audiences are in the room at every post.

The Hook Framework

A hook is the first 1 to 2 seconds of a video or the first line of a caption. Its only job is to stop the scroll. These patterns were documented in the strategy framework and used across all coaching and tour content.

  • Contrarian "Sprinting your run-up is actually slowing you down."
  • Curiosity gap "Why the fastest bowlers don't sprint at the start of their run-up."
  • Negative + specificity "Most young bowlers peak too early. Your momentum should peak here."
  • Conviction + brevity "Your run-up is killing your pace at the crease. Here's why."
  • Three fragments "Build speed. Don't start with it. The difference is everything."
  • Direct callout "Most juniors get this wrong."
  • Numbers + claim "5 cricket seasons in a row. At 17."
  • Contrast "While everyone else hibernates, our players are in the sun."

Caption Structure

The long-form feed caption template applied across all content during the internship:

[Hook: one line, stops scroll]
[Context: 1 to 2 sentences setting up the story or value]
[Body: 2 to 4 short paragraphs with actual information]
[Specifics: dates, prices, inclusions, names]
[One CTA: final line only]

09 / Reflection

Reflection

A personal reflection on the experience, skills developed, and what the internship demonstrated about working in professional marketing.

The first coaching video I posted to @cricket__for__all became their highest-viewed post to date. That result represented the anchor I based everything on over the internship: if you teach something genuinely useful, the audience shows up. I built the coaching video series around that idea — treat every post as an opportunity to deliver value first, not to sell — and the engagement data confirmed it was the right approach. It is one thing to learn content and marketing theory across three years of a marketing degree; it is another to watch the numbers change in real time when you apply it correctly.

The hardest part of the internship was not the creative work, it was the scope. I was covering for the person who normally runs CFA's social media full-time, while working roughly 17 hours a week across three days. Balancing that against the expectations of multiple stakeholders meant constantly prioritising. In hindsight, I needed to be more assertive earlier: communicate clearly what was achievable in the time available, identify the highest-impact work, and not try to do everything adequately when fewer things done well would have served the brand better. That is the clearest practical lesson I am taking forward.

Navigating Ashley's creative direction was its own learning curve. His instinct was toward direct promotional content — a post announcing a discount, a list of dates and prices. My read of the audience was different: that kind of content gets scrolled past. I had to find ways to be clear about why a different approach would perform better, without undermining the relationship or the trust he had extended to me. That tension between deferring to a supervisor and backing your own expertise is something I had not encountered in a university context. The internship made it real, and working through it built a kind of professional confidence that coursework is unable to replicate.

The culture at Cricket For All shaped the work in quieter ways. The name is the guideline: every caption had to either speak to a nine-year-old's parent in the morning or a state-pathway player in the afternoon, without losing either. That versatility made the copy more considered. It also made the environment low-pressure, which gave me room to experiment, make decisions, and develop at a pace I had not experienced in previous work.

The internship confirmed the direction. I came out of it with a clearer understanding of what I am good at — content strategy, scripting, building trust through value-driven content — and a sharper sense of what I still need to develop. That is exactly what an internship should do, and it is what this one did.
